Extended summary

Researchers at IIT-Madras and Gauhati University have developed a climate-network method that uses 84 years of rainfall data to identify when the monsoon becomes established at specific locations, distinguishing sustained rainfall from brief, localised showers. The approach treats geographical locations as nodes in a network, with links representing spatial connections between areas experiencing rainfall onset, and tracks the growth of clusters to determine when local rainfall becomes part of the largest planetary-scale monsoon system. The study found that large-scale onset occurs climatologically around May 15 over the northeast and June 9 over the Indian peninsula, marked by an abrupt increase in the size of the largest cluster followed by northward progression. Intended to complement existing operational definitions, the method could provide farmers with more reliable sowing information and assist policymakers with food security, water-resource management and flood preparedness.
