Extended summary

A University of Exeter study analyzing data from 4,219 children aged four to 11 found that those taken to parks at least once a week scored an average of 16.5 percent better on mental health assessments compared to those visiting less frequently. The research, funded by the UK Medical Research Council and NIHR, used data from the Children of the 90s study and controlled for socioeconomic status and family adversity. Researchers focused on social-emotional development, finding that more frequent park visits correlated with better skills in managing feelings, building relationships, and coping with challenges. Lead researcher Mark Ferguson stated that encouraging weekly park visits could support emotional wellbeing and mental health, provided communities have good-quality parks that are safe, accessible, and welcoming to all families. The study highlighted physical activity, social interaction, and play opportunities as key factors supporting children's healthy development.
