Extended summary

Researchers at IIT Guwahati have developed a new method for assessing seismic hazards in earthquake-prone Northeast India by compiling a catalogue of nearly 6,000 earthquakes recorded over the past 1,200 years and generating 200 synthetic earthquake catalogues. Using Kernel Density Estimation and accounting for geological fault orientations, the study addresses non-uniform seismic activity and historical data inaccuracies. The research identified the Indo-Burma Ranges as having the most intense earthquake activity, with Imphal showing the highest estimated ground shaking among eight major cities including Guwahati, Agartala, Shillong, Aizawl, Kohima, Gangtok and Itanagar. The findings provide crucial information for engineers to design earthquake-resistant buildings, bridges and infrastructure in the vulnerable region between two tectonic boundaries that was devastated by the 1897 Shillong and 1950 Assam earthquakes.
