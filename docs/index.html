A study of 38 cats found they meowed significantly more when humans were present to solve problems.University of São Paulo researchers found 38 pet cats meowed significantly more when facing an unsolvable treat puzzle with humans present than when alone, suggesting cats use vocalizations to direct human attention.5 outlets · 2026-11-15 17:18 UTC

Extended summary

Animal behaviorists at the University of São Paulo conducted a study examining communication in 38 pet cats. Researchers placed a treat inside a plastic container with holes, allowing cats to smell but not reach the food, creating a problem only humans could solve with opposable thumbs. Over one minute, the team observed cats under two conditions: with an attentive human present and with no human present. The cats were significantly more vocal when humans were nearby, with researchers finding they were four times more shouty when owners were present and 16 times more likely to meow when a person was in the room compared to being alone. Cats also stared at humans and flicked their eyes toward the container. The researchers concluded that meowing plays a communicative role in cat-human interactions, with cats deliberately targeting humans to solve problems and meet demands.
