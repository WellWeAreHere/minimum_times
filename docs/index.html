Extended summary

Archaeologists unearthed 208 Celtic silver coins dating to the late second or early first century B.C. during excavations for an electrical substation in western Hungary between 2023 and 2025. The Velem-type tetradrachms, weighing approximately 2 kilograms, were discovered inside a ceramic vessel buried 40 centimeters below ground near Szombathely. The hoard represents one of the largest such finds in Hungary in decades, with the last comparable discovery occurring in the late 1960s. Excavations also revealed Iron Age graves, stone tools dating back roughly 7,000 years, a glass bead approximately 2,500 years old, and a Roman brick bearing a dog's paw print from a rural villa complex. The site shows evidence of more than 7,000 years of human occupation.
