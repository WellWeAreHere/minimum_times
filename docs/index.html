Extended summary

Researchers at IIT Guwahati have developed a new method for seismic hazard assessment in Northeast India, compiling a catalogue of nearly 6,000 earthquakes over the past 1,200 years and generating 200 synthetic earthquake catalogues using Kernel Density Estimation and geological fault orientations. The study identified the Indo-Burma Ranges as having the most intense earthquake activity, with Imphal showing the highest estimated ground shaking among eight major cities including Guwahati, Agartala, Shillong, Aizawl, Kohima, Gangtok and Itanagar. Northeast India lies between two tectonic boundaries and contains the Shillong Plateau and numerous geological faults, making it prone to earthquakes such as the devastating 1897 Shillong and 1950 Assam events. The methodology accounts for non-uniform seismic activity and historical data inaccuracies, providing crucial information for engineers to design earthquake-resistant buildings, bridges and infrastructure in the vulnerable region.
